### About

I study how large language models fail, and how to measure that failure honestly.

I am a researcher at the Vector Institute, working in the Cyber Science Lab under the supervision of Dr. Fattane Zarrinkalam and Dr. Ali Dehghantanha. My research sits where probability theory, adversarial machine learning, and security meet.

My route here was not the usual one. Before research I spent eight years designing and delivering enterprise systems for large industrial organizations, including national scale ERP and SAP deployments. That work left me with a systems engineer's instinct for how complex systems actually break once real people and real incentives are attached to them. I now point that instinct at language models.

### Research focus

**Jailbreak attacks and defense evaluation**
Moving past binary success rates toward harm centric measurement of what multi turn attacks actually produce, so that a defense is judged by the damage it prevents rather than by a pass or fail flag.

**Retrieval augmented generation security**
Knowledge poisoning, retrieval manipulation, and the attack surface that opens the moment a model is allowed to read from an external store.

**Adversarial machine learning**
Threat modelling, attack construction, and defense evaluation under realistic constraints rather than laboratory ones.

**Probabilistic and statistical foundations**
Evaluation design, uncertainty quantification, and experimental methodology. Strong attack numbers mean nothing without a defensible measurement protocol underneath them.

### Current work

Building reproducible attack and defense evaluation pipelines for open weight and hosted language models, with the measurement protocol treated as a first class part of the research rather than an afterthought.
